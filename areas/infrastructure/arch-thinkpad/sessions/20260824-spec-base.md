# 2026-08-24 — The boundary got an assertion; a step nobody referenced turned out to be missing

Fourth round of a run that began on 2026-08-23 and crossed midnight; dated to
the day it finished, and the man pages below were read on that side of the
boundary. The first round ([[20260823-build-spec-schema|log]]) settled the
schema, the second ([[20260823-spec-boot-promotion|log]]) promoted `spec/boot`,
the third ([[20260823-spec-disk|log]]) wrote `spec/disk`; this one wrote
[[../spec/base|spec/base]] and, in the process, found that `spec/boot` had been
missing a step since it was written — then wrote that too, on a reason opposite
to the one the PRD gives for it.

Artifacts: [[../spec/base|spec/base.md]], an extended
[[../spec/boot|spec/boot.md]] (`bootloader-install`, `bootloader-nvram-entry`,
one new edge into `uki-sign`), a sixth convention in
[[../spec/_schema|_schema.md]], [[../runbooks/bootloader-reinstall|a runbook for
the §9.6 hazard]], an amended [[../spec/_index|spec/_index.md]] and
[[../runbooks/_index|runbooks/_index.md]], and one entry in
[[areas/infrastructure/arch-thinkpad/state-notes|notes.md]]. This is what did not fit in them.

## The unresolved-`requires` table cannot see a missing step

This is the finding that outlives the chapter.

Three rounds have used the count of dangling ids as the progress measure, and
the second round already learned once that it is a bad one — it had counted
*new ids* rather than *new unresolved references*, which differ whenever a
chapter satisfies what it introduces. `spec/_index.md` gained a table naming
each id and its future chapter, precisely so the number could not be wrong
again.

The number was not wrong this round. It was **silent**.

`first-boot` needs the machine to boot. Writing its `requires` list meant asking
what makes that true, and the honest answer includes a bootloader: the firmware
reaches `systemd-boot` at `/boot/EFI/systemd/systemd-bootx64.efi`, with
`/boot/EFI/BOOT/BOOTX64.EFI` as the fallback (*measured*, `state/boot.md`,
`## bootctl`). Neither file is written by any step in `spec/boot`. PRD §3.4 is
titled "Generate UKIs and install systemd-boot" and `spec/boot` promoted the
`mkinitcpio -P` half; `bootctl install` and `loader.conf` went with the other
half. `loader.conf` carries `editor no`, which
[[../spec/disk#btrfs-default-subvol|`btrfs-default-subvol`]] and
[[areas/infrastructure/arch-thinkpad/state-notes|notes.md]] both lean on when they say the cmdline cannot be
corrected from the boot menu.

And `uki-sign` **signs both of those files**. A written, reviewed, tested step
operates on two binaries that exist only because of a step nobody wrote.

The table lists ids that some `requires:` line mentions. A step that nothing
mentions is not on it, is not counted, and does not make the number look wrong.
Four-to-four would have read as no progress; four-to-two reads as progress; and
neither number contained the information. What found it was reading a PRD
section against the chapter claiming to replace it — the same mechanism that
found ADR-0014 one round ago, which is now two for two.

`bootloader-install` is referenced from `first-boot` and on the table, assigned
to `spec/boot`. PRD §4.2 suggests it may be two steps rather than one:
"`bootctl install` inside `arch-chroot` cannot write EFI variables — it only
places the loader at the removable-media fallback path," so the NVRAM half has
to happen after `first-boot` and the ESP half before it. That would put a second
straddle in a chapter that already has one.

## §2.2 strips one of two options and gives the reason for both

The task flagged the root fstab line's `subvol=` strip as belonging here, on the
understanding that PRD §2.2 performs it. **§2.2 does not.** It strips
`subvolid=` only:

```bash
sed -i 's/subvolid=[0-9]*,//g' /mnt/etc/fstab
```

and its own "expected final shape" block shows the root line as
`…,compress=zstd:1,subvol=/@`. Both places that verify this — §11's verification
block and Appendix A — grep for `subvolid` alone. The document is internally
consistent and consistently half-right.

The reason §2.2 gives is what makes it half-right rather than merely
incomplete: `subvolid=` "breaks `snapper rollback`, which creates a new
subvolume ID that a pinned fstab will not follow." A rollback creates a new
snapshot at a new *path* as well as a new id. `btrfs(5)` gives both options the
same sentence — each "overrides the default subvolume set for the given
filesystem" (*measured*, read 2026-08-24) — and overriding the default subvolume
is the entire hazard. The option name is not the part that does the damage.

`state/storage.md`'s `## fstab` shows the machine's root line carrying neither.
So the built system already does what the document does not describe, and
nothing recorded why. Third divergence of this kind in two rounds, after the two
TRIM ones.

## Deciding where the assertion goes, and the reason changing under me

The open question inherited from `spec/disk` was whether the edit deserves its
own postcondition or whether `btrfs-default-matches-root` is the right place for
it to surface. I started with the timing argument — the invariant is on the far
side of `first-boot`, so the earliest it can speak is after a reboot, one step
past the point where the fstab could still be fixed cheaply. True, and weak.

Then the invariant stopped looking reliable at all.
`systemd-remount-fs.service(8)`: it applies fstab's options "to the root file
system… This is required so that the mount options of these file systems — which
are **pre-mounted** by the kernel, the initrd, container environments or system
manager code — are **updated** to those configured in /etc/fstab" (*measured*,
read 2026-08-24). The root is remounted from fstab, not mounted from it, and the
subvolume was already chosen by the initramfs. Whether a remount carrying a
mismatched `subvol=` moves the mounted subvolume, errors, or is ignored is not
answered by `btrfs(5)`, whose `subvol` entry describes mounting. So the claim in
`btrfs-default-matches-root`'s prose — that a missed strip makes the mounted
root stop following the default subvolume, and the invariant says so — is
`inferred` and may well be wrong.

**An invariant that might not fire is worse than no coverage, because it reads
as coverage.** That settled it, and I nearly stopped there.

The argument that actually settles it is different and does not depend on any of
the above. The obvious way to write the edit is §2.2's `sed` generalised — a
global `s/,?subvol=[^,]*//` — and that takes the option off **all six** btrfs
lines. The other five mounts then resolve to the top level: five directories
holding the wrong tree, no error at any stage, and a root line that is now
correct, so `btrfs-default-matches-root` passes. The invariant could not express
that failure even in principle, because the failure is not about the root.

So the edit needed a postcondition asserting in *both* directions, and the
sharpest reason for it was one I only found by writing the naive `sed` and
looking at what came out. Same shape as the `luks-format` precondition in the
previous round: the hole was in my own first draft, and the general rule came
out of it rather than preceding it.

The transformation and the check were run against a synthesised `genfstab -U`
output for this machine's mount set and eight mutations — naive global strip,
§2.2 as written, doubled `genfstab`, appended second swap line, missing
`pri=10`, default ESP masks, no swap line, `genfstab` without `-U`. PASS on the
first, one distinct diagnostic on each of the rest. The blocks tested were
extracted from the finished document rather than retyped, after the first
version of the check produced an unreadable multi-line diagnostic on the
doubled-fstab case and had to be restructured to skip per-line checks for any
mount point whose row count was not one.

## Absence from `## explicit` does not mean absence from the machine

`base-install`'s postcondition needed a way to say "these packages are here",
and the obvious grounding is `state/packages.md`'s `## explicit`. Five of the
PRD's thirty-four pacstrap packages are not in it: `linux-headers`,
`linux-lts-headers`, `nftables`, `sudo`, `pciutils`.

`nftables` settles what that means. `nftables.service` is in
`state/services.md`'s `## system enabled` and `state/network.md`'s `## nftables`
shows a live default-drop ruleset with eight rules in it. The package is
unquestionably installed. `## explicit` is `pacman -Qqe` (*measured*,
`collect.sh` line 11), which reports the **install reason**, and the install
reason moves over a machine's life without anything about the package changing.

So a postcondition written against `-Qqe` would be red for five entries on a
correctly built machine. That is the permanently-red check `luks-format` avoids
by refusing to count keyslots, arrived at from the opposite direction: there the
trap was asserting a quantity that grows, here it is asserting a property that
decays. Both are the same underlying error — asserting something adjacent to
what the step established rather than what it established.

*(`inferred`: that the reason is `dependency` for all five. `pacman -Qi nftables
| grep 'Install Reason'` settles it and nothing depends on the answer.)*

## `--root` is the wrong flag and the man page says so

Querying the target's package database from the installer wants `pacman -r
/mnt`, which is the flag everyone reaches for. `pacman(8)` on `-r/--root`:
"**NOTE**: This option is not suitable for performing operations on a mounted
guest system. See *--sysroot* instead." And `--sysroot`: "This path will be
prepended to all other configuration directories… This allows mounted guest
systems to be properly operated on." (*measured*, read 2026-08-24.)

A mounted guest system is exactly what `/mnt` is at that point in the build.
Nothing about the `-r` name suggests the restriction, and a `-r` query would
have appeared to work. Channel 2 of the lookup rule earning its place on a flag
that nobody would have thought to check.

## An ordering requirement that survived into no chapter

PRD §3 opens with a bolded ordering requirement: §3.6 (locale, keymap,
`/etc/vconsole.conf`) must run before §3.4 generates the UKIs, because
"mkinitcpio embeds `/etc/vconsole.conf` into the initramfs at build time" — so a
first UKI built before that file exists ships with no console font. `spec/boot`'s
`uki-generate` carries no such edge, and neither does anything else. The
constraint is in a paragraph of prose in a document the spec is replacing.

I did not add the edge. The claim is **`inherited-unverified`**: §3.4 lists
`sd-vconsole: /etc/vconsole.conf not found` among its expected warnings, which
is consistent with the claim without being it, and the lookup that closes it is
`mkinitcpio -H sd-vconsole` on the machine — the same channel that closed the
hook-ordering gap last round after it had been written off as unclosable.
Editing a tested step record on the strength of an unverified sentence is the
error the vault exists to prevent, and the whole point of the previous round's
reorder was that the tool answers this in one command.

## A defect in the previous chapter, found by hitting it here

`fstab-generate`'s postcondition counts things that can legitimately be zero,
and `grep -c` exits 1 when it counts nothing. Under `set -euo pipefail` an
assignment from a failing command substitution exits the script — so the "no
swap line at all" case, which §2.2 explicitly anticipates, would have produced
no `RESULT=` line and no diagnostic. Dropping `-e` fixed it.

The same pattern is in two steps in `spec/disk`, both under `set -euo pipefail`:

```
n=$(findmnt -rno TARGET -t btrfs | grep -c '^/mnt')      # btrfs-subvol-mount
nparts=$(lsblk -lno TYPE "$DISK" | grep -cx part)        # disk-partition
```

The second is the one that matters. Zero partitions is the "`sgdisk` did
nothing" case, in the most destructive step in the spec, and the check for it
aborts the way a broken check would — a direct instance of `_schema.md`'s "a
check must not fail the same way as the thing it detects", inside a chapter that
quotes that convention twice.

Not fixed here. Those records were tested against mutation sets that were never
written down, and a one-token change to a destructive step's postcondition
should arrive with its re-test rather than ahead of it. Recorded in
`spec/base`'s open list and here.

## §4.2 was wrong in both halves, and the truth was a better argument

`bootloader-install` was written the same session, so the "check the PRD prose
before splitting on it" instinct got tested immediately. PRD §4.2:

> `bootctl install` inside `arch-chroot` cannot write EFI variables — it only
> places the loader at the removable-media fallback path.

**"Only the fallback path" is false.** `bootctl(1)`: install "Installs
**systemd-boot** into the EFI system partition. A copy of **systemd-boot** will
be stored as the EFI default/fallback loader at *ESP*/EFI/BOOT/BOOT\*.EFI"
(*measured*, read 2026-08-24). Both locations, and `state/boot.md` lists both
files under "Available Boot Loaders on ESP".

**"Cannot write EFI variables" is not established either, and the option that
would do the suppressing draws the line somewhere else.** `--variables=`: "If
not specified or set to **auto**, EFI variables will not be modified when
execution in a **container runtime** is detected." A chroot is not a container
runtime and the flag distinguishes them on purpose. There is also no
`--no-variables` — the flag is `--variables=yes|no|auto`, added in version 258 —
so the one flag that would have existed "precisely for this case" is not the one
that exists.

**What is true is worse than what §4.2 claims, and it is why there are two
steps.** `--graceful`: "Ignore failure when the EFI System Partition cannot be
found, when EFI variables cannot be written, or a different or newer boot loader
is already installed… **Since version v258, it is implicitly enabled when
running inside a chroot.**" The machine runs systemd 261.2. So inside the chroot
`bootctl install` exits 0 whether or not the variable was written, and whether
or not it found an ESP at all.

§4.2 says the chroot *cannot* do it. The truth is that it tries, may succeed,
and hides the answer either way. Both readings produce two steps; only the
second explains why the second step is needed **even when the first succeeded**.
A split built on §4.2's reasoning would have been right by accident and would
have carried a false sentence into the spec.

## Asking the tool produced the right verb and the environment had disabled it

`bootctl is-installed` is exactly the verb these two steps want, and the man page
draws the line for them: it "Checks whether **systemd-boot** is installed in the
ESP… this hence checks whether **systemd-boot** is one (of possibly many)
installed boot loaders — and neither whether it is the default nor whether it is
registered in any EFI variables" (*measured*, read 2026-08-24). That sentence is
the two postconditions, written by upstream.

It is also one of the four verbs `--graceful` covers, so in a chroot it returns
success when the ESP cannot be found — the same silent success as the command it
would be checking.

The lookup rule says ask the tool first, and that was right: the tool named the
distinction. But channel 1 would have produced `bootctl is-installed` as the
postcondition, and only the man page says the environment has switched it off.
**The two channels were not alternatives here; the second one was needed to know
whether to trust the first.** That is a nuance on the ordering rather than a
counterexample to it.

## An ordering hazard that was not in the brief

`bootctl install` copies binaries into the ESP. Running it after `uki-sign`
therefore replaces `systemd-bootx64.efi` and `BOOTX64.EFI` with unsigned copies
— the second member of the class `uki-sign`'s prose already names for
regenerated UKIs, and with a worse ending, because a firmware that rejects its
own loader offers no menu to recover from. The fix is an edge:
`uki-sign requires bootloader-nvram-entry`, so the NVRAM re-run is ordered
before signing rather than left to whoever reads the chapter top to bottom.
`secureboot-enable`'s precondition, which re-verifies every file at run time,
is the backstop for a human arriving out of order.

**PRD §9.6 walks straight into it.** Its recovery row for "Firmware update
bricked boot entries" is `bootctl install` from the ISO chroot, then
`mkinitcpio -P`, with no re-signing step. On a Secure Boot machine that
procedure leaves the firmware rejecting its own loader: the repair for a broken
boot entry breaks the boot outright. That one is runbook-shaped rather than
spec-shaped — §9.6 is a table of what to do when something is already broken,
and this chapter has no step for that state — so it is recorded and not fixed.

## A heredoc that would only have worked in one of its two readings

`bootloader-install` writes `loader.conf`, and the first draft used
`cat > … <<'EOF'` with the body and terminator at the block's own indentation.
Inside a YAML literal block scalar that is fine: the common indentation is
stripped and `EOF` lands at column zero.

It is not fine anywhere else, and everywhere else is how this text actually gets
used. `_schema.md`'s unit of transclusion is the *section*, and a reader copying
a command out of a rendered page copies its leading whitespace with it — at
which point the terminator no longer matches and the shell either hangs or
writes two-space-indented keys, so that the step's own
`grep -qE '^editor[[:space:]]+no$'` fails on a correct run. `<<-` does not help;
it strips tabs, not spaces.

Every other command in this vault survives that treatment because shell ignores
leading whitespace before a command. A heredoc is the one construct that does
not. Replaced with `printf '%s\n'` and a quoted argument per line, which is
indentation-immune. **This may be worth a sixth convention in `_schema.md`** —
a step record's command must survive being copied out with its indentation —
since it comes from a specific failure like four of the five already there, but
it is small enough that adding it is a judgment call rather than an obvious one.

## Two things deliberately left out of the chapter

**`passwd -l root`.** §3.5 lays out both sides at length and closes "Either
choice is defensible; make it deliberately rather than by copying this line."
`state/` cannot say which way this machine went — nothing collects `passwd -S
root`. Writing it into `user-create` would record a choice nobody made, in the
artifact whose job is to record intent accurately. It wants an ADR, and the
collector change that would let the ADR cite current state is one line.

**Four of five groups.** `useradd -m -G wheel,audio,video,storage,input` sets
five and the chapter asserts one. `wheel` is tied to an observable through the
sudo grant; the other four have no grounding in `state/`, which records no group
membership at all. Marked `inferred` and the lookup named, rather than asserted
because the PRD says so.

## The sudo check that replaced a grep

§3.5 verifies the grant with `grep -c '^%wheel ALL=(ALL:ALL) ALL' /etc/sudoers`,
which is asserting against a constant three times over: it misses a grant
arriving through `/etc/sudoers.d`, it is sensitive to whitespace the parser is
not, and it says nothing about whether the file parses at all. Two live readings
replace it — `visudo -c`, one of the few documented exit statuses in this vault
("If the check completes successfully, `visudo` will exit with a value of 0"),
and `sudo -l -n -U luiswg <command>`, where "the exit value will only be 0 if
the command is permitted by the security policy" (*measured*, both read
2026-08-24).

`-n` is there for the executor rather than for correctness: "Avoid prompting the
user for input of any kind." A postcondition that can block on a password prompt
is a postcondition that hangs a build instead of failing it.

**The thirteen split into two classes, and the second is the dangerous one.**
Six are wrong facts — rows 1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10 — and a wrong fact announces itself
the moment anything is checked against it. The other seven are *things that
would have looked like they worked*: an invariant that may not fire (2), a
justification that would have covered the wrong failure (3), a progress measure
that reads plausibly while omitting a whole class (6, 7), an exit status
documented to be uninformative (11), the tool's own check verb disabled by its
environment (12), and a rescue procedure that completes successfully against the
wrong filesystem (13). Nothing downstream of any of those reports anything.

Row 13 is the cleanest specimen of the class. `arch-chroot` into `@` succeeds,
`sbctl` is present, `sign-all` runs and prints what it signed, `exit` and
`reboot` behave — and the machine is still broken, because the key store and
signing database that were consulted belong to a root the machine does not boot.
Every individual step reports success. There is no point at which anything is
wrong enough to say so.

Three matter most, one from each part of the round:

- **Row 1.** A premise carried into the task from the previous round's open list
  and never checked against the document it named. It was in the brief, it was
  plausible, and one `grep` for `subvol` in the PRD refuted it. Inherited
  premises need the same treatment as inherited claims, which is what the
  vault's `inherited-unverified` marker exists to say and what a task
  description does not carry.
- **Row 6.** The measure three rounds have leaned on, failing for the second
  time and in the harder direction — the previous round it produced a wrong
  number, this round a right-looking one. A measure that can be silently
  incomplete is worse than one that can be wrong.
- **Row 11.** The only correction that changed a step's *structure* rather than
  a claim about it. §4.2's reason for splitting `bootctl install` is false;
  `--graceful` being implicit in a chroot is a different and stronger reason for
  the same split, and it is the one that explains why the second step is needed
  **even when the first succeeded**. Had I taken §4.2 at its word the shape
  would have been right and the argument would have been fiction — the failure
  mode that is hardest to notice later, because the artifact looks correct.

Worth noting how rows 8–12 arrived: five corrections out of one man page read
against three sentences of PRD prose, in one sitting. The density is not a
coincidence. §4.2's sentence is the kind that gets written once from a plausible
mental model and then never re-derived, and everything downstream of it inherits
the model rather than the measurement.

**The PRD sections found defective this round were §2.2, §3.9, §4.2, §9.5 and
§9.6** — an incomplete `sed`, a `swapoff` ordered after the `umount` that should
follow it, two false clauses in one sentence, a rescue chroot on the wrong
subvolume, and a recovery row that breaks a Secure Boot machine. Naming sections
rather than counting defects, because §4.2 alone contributes two and the total
depends on how you slice it.

What they have in common is more useful than the number: **four of the five are
in §§9–12, the recovery and deferred material**, which is the part of the
document nobody re-reads because nothing routinely exercises it. §§1–3 have been
read in every round of this work and are in comparatively good shape. The
sections that describe what to do when the machine is broken are the ones most
likely to be wrong, and they are the ones read under the least favourable
conditions. That is the argument for `runbooks/` existing at all, arrived at
from the evidence rather than from the premise.

## Still open

- **`bootloader-reinstall` has not been rehearsed.** It is written from man
  pages and from `state/`, and its fault-B path — the firmware refusing an
  unsigned loader — has never been observed on this machine; that symptom is
  marked `inferred` in the runbook. The ISO-chroot half cannot be rehearsed
  without an ISO and a willingness to break the boot on purpose. PRD §9.6 closes
  with "Keep this table honest by rehearsing at least the snapper rollback path
  once", which is the right instinct pointed at a different row.
- **`spec/containers`**, and the chapters covering PRD §3.6–§3.8 and beyond,
  which have no names yet. §3's vconsole ordering requirement belongs to one of
  them and currently exists nowhere.
- **The two `grep -c` aborts in `spec/disk`**, with their re-test.
- **Whether root is locked on this machine**, which nothing measures, and the
  ADR that should record the choice.
- **`discard=async` is in `## mounts` and not in `## fstab`**, while every other
  kernel-reported default that argued the genfstab case is in both. Unexplained.
- **Nothing in `spec/base` has been executed.** `fstab-generate` was tested
  against synthesised input and eight mutations, which is the strongest form
  available under the standing limitation from the second round; `first-boot`'s
  postcondition has been run in none of the four namespaces it distinguishes.
