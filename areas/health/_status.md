## Key learnings

- **Work-schedule physiology:** the 12-hour shifts created non-standard recovery windows, and high step counts counted as real training volume. *(Written for the superseded shift pattern; re-check against the current schedule.)*
- **Keto-to-carb transition:** electrolyte needs change a lot. The 5 g sodium that keto needed becomes excessive once carbohydrates return, because the kidneys handle sodium differently. Titrate down.
- **Hypertrophy vs. strength:** StrongLifts 5x5 builds strength, not mass. The hypertrophy goal needed an explicit program change in volume, rep ranges and exercise selection.
- **Equipment constraints:** rings and bodyweight progressions can drive substantial hypertrophy when programmed well. The limit is the progressive-overload ceiling, not the stimulus.

## Approach

- One file per health domain. Measurements carry timestamps and context. Protocol changes are logged with their rationale. Sources say whether they are peer-reviewed, mechanistic reasoning or anecdotal/n=1.
- Change one variable at a time when possible: baseline, intervention, enough time, conclusion. Avoid stacking protocols that hide causation.
- **Review cadence:** training program every 8 weeks or at deload; supplement stack quarterly; bloodwork at least yearly, more often when testing interventions.
