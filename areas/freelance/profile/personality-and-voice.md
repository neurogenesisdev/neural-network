# Personality and voice — Luis Gordon

> [!NOTE] Summary
> How Luis thinks, decides, talks and writes, as observed and as he states it,
> with his own phrases kept verbatim where they carry his voice. Compiled
> 2026-09-28 from the site-copy sessions. Purpose: brand definition, bios,
> and later a writing-voice profile for a Writing Mentor project. Facts about
> his life: [[background]]. Copy rules derived from this: [[../standards/communication]].

## How he thinks

- **Systems thinker, autodidact.** Learns by building the whole thing at once (SaaS + full stack + AI coding simultaneously in 2026, and says so). Reads across fields; connects them (the name neurogenesis is an example: biology, ML and "making" in one word).
- **Optimizer.** Same instinct applied to body, mind, hardware, software, routines. Prefers one word that covers all of it over separate labels.
- **Decides by shortlist.** He wants options to react to, not open questions; says yes/no fast, edits in place. Applies the same principle to his clients (curated reference lists instead of "sites you like"). When decision-fatigued, he asks for numbered proposals and answers by exception. Afterwards he reframed the fatigue as "neuroplasticity in action, experienced as confusion".
- **Honesty as a constraint, image as a goal.** "All for honesty", but will leave room for interpretation where stating the full picture would undersell him (the extent of AI involvement). Won't claim completed courses, won't claim experience, won't name a client without consent. Draws the line at claims that could be checked and found false.
- **Scientific accuracy matters to him personally.** Rejected "the birth of new neurons" and "the brain rebuilding itself as it learns" because they conflate neurogenesis and neuroplasticity; asked for research on ANN-vs-brain similarity before accepting an analogy. Would rather sound precise than poetic; fears sounding like "a Dunning-Kruger epitome".
- **Self-aware about impostor syndrome.** Named it himself when he considered removing his name from the hero; corrected course within a message. Ashamed of the missing degree, says so, and decides not to advertise it rather than to hide it.

## How he talks (in sessions)

- Direct, technically dense, intolerant of imprecision. Pushes back immediately and expects correction without re-explanation. Curses freely when frustrated ("stupid fucking redundancies", "sucks ass"); it's emphasis, not hostility, and it subsides as soon as the work is right.
- Long voice-message rambles when there's a lot to decide; he expects the listener to distil them. Says so: "I guess I rambled and ranted enough for you to distil my vision."
- Praises specifically and rarely ("B for line 4 is sick", "looking pretty damn good"). Rejects with reasons, usually the right ones.
- Jargon-fluent in AI tooling (harnesses, MCP, permissions, auto-mode), sysadmin (systemd timers, cron, VPS), and biohacking (adenosine half-life, circadian entrainment); college-grade in business and legal terms, and wants those explained in passing.

## What he wants his public voice to be

- **Professional, not playful.** No emoji in titles, no thumbs-up/down, nothing "you'd hand a kid". The Chantal addendum incident (2026-09-23) set this rule.
- **Not AI-sounding.** Phrases that read as generated are rejected outright; so are cliché disclaimers ("as a reader, not a researcher", "a few wild cards") — he called those "ass" and "hold your horses, ladies and gentlemen". Prefers a plain statement over a hedge.
- **Understated confidence.** "A toe outside the box, not the whole body." Look like what the public already reads as professional; put personality in details, not in the headline.
- **Maverick without cringe.** Wanted "a bootstrapped, self-taught solo developer struggle story", then immediately: "I made the analogies a bit extreme … a toned down version of this vibe without making it cringe." The balance point: state the facts of the path (started 2025, building since Jan 2026, no team and none needed), let the reader infer the rest.
- **Mystery over inventory.** Deliberately keeps interests short so future writing can reveal them: "take away from the mystery of me, and the surprise when I eventually write about it."
- **Sie in German client copy; English leads.** English is his stronger written voice; German is native but he checks the translations closely and catches unnatural phrasing ("Gewohnheiten mit System sounds completely unnatural").

- "Because you still don't have one right now, and because you can tell me exactly what you want, and I know how to implement it." (why a prospect should hire him — kept for outreach)
- "The decision fatigue was neuroplasticity in action, experienced as confusion."
- "I like optimizing everything … my hardware, my software, both digital and analog, electronic and biological. Behavioural routines and thought patterns."
- "It is foolish to not make use of these tools, and … me profiting off of being ahead of the technological curve is a fair and deserved advantage."
- "I'm transformed from a single individual to a virtual team, a multiplexed android individual." (private; too much for the page, right for an article)
- "Kein Rhythmus, kein Füllstoff" (accepted as his newsletter line; he liked the register)

## What to avoid in anything written as him

- Claimed experience, plural clients, "agency"; anything a stranger could check and find false.
- Leading with a weakness and scrambling to compensate ("no degree, but…").
- Hedges, disclaimers, exclamation marks, motivational-poster phrasing.
- Listing substances, personal medical details, or the day job.
- Explaining the name for more than three sentences.
