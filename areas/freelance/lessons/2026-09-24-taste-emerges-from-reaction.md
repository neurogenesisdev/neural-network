# Taste emerges from reaction, not from the brief

> [!NOTE] Summary
> During round one of the neurogenesis.dev redesign (2026-09-21 → 24) Luis
> was the client and the Architect + Claude Code were the builders. A detailed
> brief still produced pages Luis rejected hard (T4 hero gradient, T5
> header/bands/footer). Each rejection surfaced a precise preference he could
> not have written down in advance; one reference-driven reset (T5b) then
> landed. Clients will behave the same way. Seed of the planned client
> workflow guide — see "Backlog" below.

## What happened

- Brief said "Linear-style", "sections with distinct backgrounds", "Huberman-style logo box". Adjectives and named sites were read differently by each link in the chain.
- Three gradient passes on the hero moved numbers, not the feel — until a reference screenshot sat in the repo and the prompt cited measured values.
- The Architect added details Luis never asked for (offer line under the logo, rounded corners, white logo text) — "helpful" interpretation widened the gap and cost a full round.
- Luis's reaction to the T5 result ("ass, ass, ass") produced the most precise spec of the whole round: square blue box, a colour per band, centred content, one booking button, footer structure — each item pointing at a reference screenshot.
- The reset built that spec literally, with the agent screenshotting and judging its own output against the references. Luis: "much better, no longer want to restart the site."
- Luis's own read: the frustration was the moment he found out what he actually wants; the brief was not precise and opinionated enough because he didn't know yet.

## Rules extracted (for client work)

1. **Show something cheap and early.** A rough first version gives the client something to react to; reaction is where taste appears. Plan for it, don't hope the brief is complete.
2. **Discovery runs on references, not open questions.** Curated sites per niche, 👍/👎 + one line each, plus a short comparison checklist (first screen, nav, colour, type, density, motion, photos). This is inventory v2 (Chantal struggled with exactly the open "sites you like/dislike" question).
3. **One reaction round is part of the fixed price** — step 4 of the process (preview link + weekly written update) is where it happens. The first "I hate this" is a planned step, not a crisis or scope creep.
4. **Build the client's words literally first; opinions go in a separate line.** If they're wrong, the preview shows it faster than an argument; if they're right, no round is wasted. Never fold your own interpretation silently into the build.
5. **Every reaction becomes a written rule** ("links on light bands: dark text, underlined, never blue"). The client's rule list is their style guide — keep it in their project note and hand it over.
6. **Specs cite a reference image and measured values, never adjectives.** Store references with the project; builders (human or agent) match images, not words.
7. **Expect the client's "ugh, no" to feel like Luis's did** — it is progress. Acknowledge it, extract the rules, rebuild.
