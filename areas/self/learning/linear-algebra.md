Vector = magnitude + direction (velocity)
Scalar = magnitude (speed)

Vectors with the same magnitude and direction are equal regardless of location in space.

Example vector notation:
$$
\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix}5 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}
$$
This vector moves 5 positive units horizontally and 0 units vertically.

$\mathbb{R}^2$  is the 2-dimensional real coordinate space, i.e. all possible real-valued 2-tuples.
$$
\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix}5 \\ 0\end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^2
$$
$\mathbb{R}^3$  is the 3-dimensional real coordinate space, i.e. all possible real-valued 3-tuples.
$$
\vec{x} = \begin{pmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^3
$$
$\mathbb{R}^n$  is the n-dimensional real coordinate space.

To add the vectors (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂), we add the corresponding components from each vector:

$\vec{a} = \begin{pmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{pmatrix}$    $\vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix}-4 \\ 4\end{pmatrix}$

$\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \begin{pmatrix}2 \\ 2\end{pmatrix}$
