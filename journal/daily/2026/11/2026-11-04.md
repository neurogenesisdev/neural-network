## Plan

I definitely suffer from dead butt syndrome, or perhaps I have suffered, and I'm beginning to address it. For the last couple of days, I've been doing glute bridges and using resistance bands to increase the resistance.

I've noticed that my overall energy and mood have been improving, as well as knee pain and ankle/foot pain reducing. My shift yesterday felt easier. Just standing for multiple hours at a time didn't put such a strain on my body. I entirely felt much more relaxed and literally as if my brain functioned better, which is interesting.

I also suspect that the lack of glute activation development is one of the primary causes of me finding it so hard to develop upper body muscle volume and strength. It's like all the energy is being lost at that midsection point and cannot be conducted up any further. Yes, that's something I'm adding to my strength routine daily.

Next thought: I have been hesitating on furthering the web design freelance business, as the actual visual design component of client work (and even my own website) is daunting to me. I don't have prior experience as a UI/UX developer. I haven't used Figma or any of the visual Adobe products before for this purpose. Even more importantly, I don't know which AI tools are available for expediting the process and reverse-engineering existing website status.

Something I'd like to hone in on is using example websites instead of just providing the links to them, feeding them into tailored AI tools that extract the likely code from example screenshots. For videos, and then 3D background designs that I could use as wallpapers or transition effects would be very nice to have and would make the mind-to-code process more natural. 

Another thing: I think neck stretches are very important, especially with my job, standing, and as a runner. As someone who sits at the laptop a lot when they're not exercising or at work, I find that sitting on a bench or a table (where the arms can be extended far to the left or right for anchoring) is better than sitting on a high chair and reaching further below to anchor. The arm hanging straight down is ideal. What one wants is to stretch and expand one's posture, not just vertically but also horizontally.

Not sure what the exact biomechanics behind that is, but there's also a phenomenon I noticed: when stretching the front muscles of the neck, it's better to have sort of a posterior tilt of the pelvis and focus on front abdominal core development. While stretching the back muscles of the neck, it's better to have an anterior pelvic tilt, focusing more on your back stabilization.

Can get more into where exactly the hands are placed and when. Thumb plus forefinger (index finger) pushing instead of pulling motion must be inflexible to be used, but I kind of intuitively know that now.
