I almost woke up with my alarm today. Instead of it being a subconscious, basically automated, still-in-sleep occurring process, I was acutely aware of the alarm being activated. After turning it off, I distinctly remember thinking to myself that this would be the ideal moment to fulfill the first habit item in my tracker, but couldn't bring myself to do it. It's a step forward. I think maybe I should adjust the alarm by half an hour to shift into a circadian cycle that more aligns with my wakeful, supposed-to-sleep state.

Yesterday evening, I did almost my entire hygiene routine:
- Brushed my teeth
- Scraped my tongue
- Washed my face
- Applied the retinol serum and microbiome-improving hydration cream
- Plaid
- No strip
- Mouth tape
- Earplugs
- Sleep mask


Obviously, I used screens late, but it's basically unavoidable. I should get some blue light-blocking glasses on top of my blue light filters on my devices, but my sleep recovery score, according to Whoop, is still only yellow, despite all the improvements that I've been applying.

It's probably due to the high training load, or at least the endurance part of it, combined with the fact that I haven't been stretching consistently. Even David fucking Goggins, who completely overkills his training routine, stretches for like 5 hours a day to compensate for the work he does. At least I can make a sacrifice for half an hour in the evening.

I did the runner's stretch, the active version of the front hip flexor, and I guess glutes in the front leg. It felt much better than the passive version against the wall or freestanding with the back leg grabbed for an additional stretch in the back hip flexor. Need to do that more often, probably after every run, and definitely every evening 

Thinking of adding neck strength training either to the strength routine or as a separate item in the habit tracker. Additionally, neck stretches and this runner's lunge, but perhaps they should just be added to a general stretching circuit that I can apply in the evening or after my workout sessions.
