### Birth and Infancy

I was born on June 5, 1995, in Kiel, Germany. My birth story begins slightly earlier, when my German mother, Dorte Arp, and my American father, William Arthur Gordon, married in Laboe, Germany after learning they were expecting me. My mother took on the Gordon surname, becoming Dorte Gordon.

When I came into the world, my father was deployed on military duty in the United States and couldn't witness my birth. Instead, my mother was surrounded by her family—my German relatives—as she welcomed me, Luis William Gordon, into the world. 

As a birth present I was gifted multiple stuffed animals, but one specifically, gifted to me by my maternal grandmother was Teddy, a stuffed white bear in white overalls and ribbon tie around his neck, about the size of my newborn self, that quickly come to hold great value to me and to whom I would form a strong attachment.

### First Home and Early Development

Shortly after my birth, we relocated to the United States, settling in El Paso, Texas, where we lived for approximately two years. From what my parents have told me, I was a remarkably quiet infant who rarely cried and was relatively easy to care for. During my infancy, I briefly battled meningitis but fortunately made a complete recovery.

It was in El Paso where my earliest personal memories begin to form. I recall playing with Legos, building castles, and finishing puzzles with my father, as well as attending Gymboree play classes for toddlers. By all accounts, I was a bright, curious, and playful child. I formed a friendship with a girl my age who lived nearby.

I am told that one day we after we had spent the day shopping and running errands, we came home or simply got into the car, I can't remember, and discovered that along the way we had lost Teddy. I was inconsolable, distressed, and in tears, completely unlike me usual self, and while my parents where discussing the possibility of buying me a replacement I insisted on finding my Teddy and not accepting any other. We retraced our steps for the better part of the afternoon, visiting multiple stores before eventually finding him in the lost and found at a Target we had visited. I was ecstatic and incredibly relieved and never lost my stuffed animal ever again from that day onwards. A fun fact, my mother used to retell about how I used to pronounce the name of the retail store Target, as Targé with a french accent which she found funny and adorable.

### Family Expansion and Transitions

As our time in Texas continued, my mother became pregnant with my younger brother. Growing weary of life in the United States, my mother initiated another move—this time to Noblesville, Indiana, where my brother Jacob Gordon was born on November 12, 1997. I have clear memories of holding my infant brother, feeling proud and protective in my new role as an older sibling.

Our stay in Indiana was brief. Shortly after Jacob's birth, we returned to Germany, settling in Nußloch, near Heidelberg. Just as we were establishing ourselves there, my father received military deployment orders to Kosovo, where he would remain for several months until just before I was old enough to begin Kindergarten in the fall of 1998, when I was three years old.

### Early Challenges

During my father's deployment, my mother effectively became a single parent, caring for both me as a toddler and my infant brother. This period was particularly challenging for her as she faced multiple stressors simultaneously: managing two young children alone, living in a house that felt too confined for her comfort and was situated alongside a busy street, and coping with her lifelong genetic retinopathy that caused her vision to become increasingly impaired. The accumulation of these challenges weighed heavily on her during this formative time in our family's life.

Despite this I can recall fond memories of playing in our backyard with the waterway play set, shovels and buckets in the sandpit, Hot Wheels racecars on my town themed carpet, soap bubbles, superhero action figures, parachute toy soldiers, and of course my dear friend Teddy.

There was instance where I used the toilet by myself for one of the first times, locked myself into the bathroom, and couldn't figure out how to turn the key to unlock the door once I was finished or perhaps it was stuck somehow. I remember feeling increasingly distressed the longer I tried to unlock the door and eventually began crying while my mother tried to talk me through the process and becoming frustrated. She ended up calling over a neighbor over for help climbed through the bathroom window and got me out. 

The most notable and traumatic memory I have of this time was when my mother decided to punish me for something I supposedly did. I do not remember whether I actually misbehaved or what exactly I did to incite her frustration and anger, but the punishment without a doubt went beyond what would have been a reasonable response considering my age.. She decided to lock me in the dark, dank, dusty, and only partly renovated basement, turned off lights before she left, and didn't tell me when she would let me out. I recall her dragging me downstairs while I was crying and begging her not to do this and vividly remember how cold, harsh, unreasonable, and unreceptive to my pleas to reconsider she was. When I was alone I was scared, confused, and continued crying, unable to find the light switch or perhaps she had turned off the electrical switch for the basement, I don't remember. I don't know how long I was down there but eventually I stopped crying and resigned myself to waiting for however long it took, until she let me out, and simply sitting in the darkness. Eventually she did let me out and seemed surprisingly regretful and sorry for how she had dealt with me, but I after experiencing that other side of her I was wary of accepting this repentance and attempts to console me and generally being despondent for a while. This was the first time I experienced the emotional volatility and emotional instability of mother and this behavior would later turn out to not just be on off occurrence but an integral part of her personality and form a pattern throughout my life and hers. 

Also worth mentioning is that one of our neighbors had a son around my age or slightly older that was battling with cancer, leukemia. We didn't interact too often except for in passing by, but I remember years later receiving the news that he had died due to the disease. This was the first time I was confronted with death, though not in my immediate family, nevertheless of someone I knew.
