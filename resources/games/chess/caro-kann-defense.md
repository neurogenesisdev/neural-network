# Caro-Kann Defense

# Foundation

- This opening is a response to e4.
- The move e4 opens up lines for the Bishop and Queen.
- The first move of this opening for black is c6.
- A few of the downsides of this move are that the preferred developing square for the Knight is blocked, and that the Queen is the only piece for which a line is opened up, although only towards the edge of the board which is suboptimal.
- The second move for black is to play d5 with the idea of striking back at the center of the board and pressuring the e4 square.

# Variations

White’s second move can be:

- d4.
    - followed by capturing the d5 pawn. This is unfavorable for white because after black retakes with the c6 pawn white is left with only one center pawn, while black has two remaining.
        - followed by c4.
    - followed by Knight to c3.
    - followed by f3.
    - followed by e5 (the advanced variation).
- Knight to c3.
    - followed by Knight to f3.

## Two Knights Variation

- After 1. e4 c6, 2. Nc3 d5, 3. Nf3 the best move here is Bg4 to prevent trapping the Bishop.
    - A weird line can happen after 4. exd5 cxd5, 5. Ne5 Bxd1, 6. Bb5 (check). This easily refuted with Nb6 and 7. Nc6 Qb6.
- Next after 4. h3 play Bxf3 and capture the Knight.
- After white captures the Bishop with 5. Qxf3 comes e6.
    - If white plays 6. d4 then dxe4, 7. Nxe4 Qxd4 is unrefuted, albeit risky. The safer option would be Nf6.
    - After 7. e5 Nfd7, black will play c5 and Nc6 and push the white pawn center and gains an advantage.
- After white’s best move 6. d3 develop the Knight with Nd7.
    - Alternatively develop the Bishop with Bb4.
- 7. a3 g6 follows.
- Then 8. g3 Bg7.
- After 9. Bg2 play Ne7
    - or Nf6.
- Then castle either short
    - or long.

## Knight e2 (Advanced) Variation

- After 1. e4 c6, 2. Ne2 d5. 3. e5 the recommended move is d4, followed by c5, Nc6, h5, and Bf5.

## b3 Variation

- After 1. e4 c6, 2. b3 d5, 3. Bb2 dxe4, 4. Qe2 Nf6 black is simply up a pawn and can easily defend.

## d3, Nd2 Variation

- 1. e4 c6, 2. d3 d5, 3. Nd2 sets up a King’s Indian attack.
- Because the center is uncontested e5 is the best move.
- What follows can look like 4. Nf3 Bd6, 5. d4 exd4, 6. exd5 and then black can play Nf6.
- After 7. dxc6 retake with Nc6, which results in a two piece lead in development.

## Fantasy Variation

- e4 c6, 2. d4 d5, 3. f3.
- White here takes the whole center and defends it with the principled move f3.
- Drawbacks for white are opening up the g1-a7 diagonal and blocking the f3 square.
- In response to white opening up the kingside pawn structure black should take with dxe4.
- After 4. fxe4 immediately re-occupy the center with e5.
    - After 5. dxe5 this also threatens Qh4 (check).
- White then plays 5. Nf3 which protects hf and d4.
- Next black should play Bg4.
    - An alternative is Be6.
    - If white then takes with 6. dxe5 black can trade queens and prevent white from ever castling.
- The dream setup for black afterwards is achieved by Nf6, Nd7, Bd6, 0-0, and Re8.
    - After move five white often plays 6. Bc4, although c3 is best. Black should defend with Nd7.
    - If instead black plays Nf6 white can play Bxf7 followed by Nxe5 it results in two consecutive checks, the capture of two pawns, and gaining gaining back a bishop by creating a fork on the king and dark square bishop.
    - After 7. 0-0 Nf6 is best, which sets up Bd6.
    - After 6. Bc4 Nd7, 7. c3 Nf6, 8. Qb3 attacking the f7 square again is a threat. The response is Bh5, which allows white to take on b7 or castle.
    - After 9. 0-0 b5 is a good move to prevent Qxb7.
    - After 10. Be2 Bd6, 11. a4 white has an advantage because then a6, 12. axb5 axb5, 13. Ra8 Qa8, 14. dxe5 Nxe5, 15. Nxe5 Bxe5, 16. Bxh5 Nxh5, white has a brutal fork with 17. Rf5.
- Black responds to 6. Bc4 with Bh5, white castles 7. 0-0 , black develops Nf6/
    - A weaker follow up for white is 8. Nd2 matched by Bd6.
    - If white plays 9. Nc4 responded to by Nc5, and then takes with 10. dxc5, black delivers a check with Bxc5 and uncovers an attack on the white bishop at the same time.
- Better for white is 8. Bg5 and black Bd6, what follows is 9. Nd2 0-0.
