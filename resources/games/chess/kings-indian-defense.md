# King’s Indian Defense

# 1. Introduction

The King’s Indian Defense is a response to white’s opening move d4. Black allows white to take control of the center to prepare a kingside attack, which involves setting up a dark square fianchetto, and closing off the center. It is one of the best openings for black to play for a win and involves sharp play.

# 2. Ideas

- Black sets up a fianchetto bishop on the h8-a1 diagonal.
- Black castles kingside and gains shelter behind the fianchetto structure.
- Black engages the center with the thematic e5 or c5 pawn pushes.

# 3. Opening

The basic King’s Indian structure is reached after:

1. d4 Nf6 
2. c4 g6
3. Nc3 Bg7
4. e4 d6

# 4. Main Line

The start of the main line for most variations look this:

1. Nf3 O-O
2. Be2 e5
3. O-O Nc6
4. d5 Ne7

## 4.3. Exchange Variation

The aggressive move e5 is a viable move for black due to the discovered threat of the fianchetto bishop:

1. dxe5 dxe5
2. Qxd8 Rxd8

White can then either take the knight which is met by:

1. Nxe5 Nxe4

or take a pawn and go for a fork on the rook which is met by:

1. Nxf7 Bxc3
2. bxc3 Kxf7
