# The Scotch Game

Opening Setup:

1. e4 e5, 2. Nf3 Nc6, 3. d4 

It is favorable for white if black retakes the pawn with the knight and loses it to the queen.

1. Continuation:

 3. d4 exd4, 4. Nxd4 Bc5, 5. Be3

After bringing out the bishop black can secure it by it retreating it one square.

2. Continuation:

5. Be3 Bb6, 6. Nf5 Bxe3, 7. Nxe3 

After white threatens the g7 pawn with the knight and black exchanges bishops, white can retake with the knight while also preventing the d4 pawn break from being played.

3. Continuation:

7. Nxe3 Nf6, 8. Nc3 O-O, 9. Qd2 Re8, 10. f3 d6, 11. O-O-O

Both sides continue development and reinforcing the center pawns by bringing out the knights and f3 and d6 pawns, followed by castling.

 

1.1. Continuation:

5. Be3 Qf6, 6. c3 Nge7, 7. Bc4 O-O, 8. O-O d6, 9. 

After black brings out the queen, white defends its attack on the knight by bringing out the pawn to c3 at the cost of blocking the b knights most natural developing square. Black responds with Nge7 due to having its developing square blocked by the queen, and protects the other knight.
